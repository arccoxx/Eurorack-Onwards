## Deutsch 1991 (the circuit-relevant one)
- Allow probabilistic/quantum states, not just deterministic.

## Aaronson & Watrous 2009
- If CTCs exist: classical and quantum computers equivalent at PSPACE (polynomial space).

## Brun 2002 (at IAS)
- CTC computer could factor large numbers, attack NP-complete problems by sending results into its own past.

## Aaronson computability result
- CTCs give exactly Δ₂ — Turing-reducible to the halting problem. Quantum CTCs buy nothing more.

## Honest physical limit
- A feedback loop is a classical analogue of a CTC but does NOT automatically enforce Deutsch's fixed-point condition.
- You get a stable attractor, not PSPACE.
- Closest desktop analogue: toroidal waveguide sim on GitHub (spin-biased FDTD waves in closed loop with rotating index perturbation) — classical wave physics, not quantum.
